2. What Heads Up Home does

Heads Up Home collects the public recall and safety notices published by the UK's food, product, medicine, veterinary medicine and workplace safety organisations, and product safety warnings published by the European Commission. It tells you about the ones that match the categories, allergens and cupboard items you choose.

Heads Up Home is independent. It isn't run by, or connected to, any of those organisations.

3. What it can't do

  • It can't promise to catch every recall, or to tell you straight away. We check for new notices every hour and match them as carefully as we can. But notices can be published late, changed, or worded in ways we can't match. Alerts also depend on your phone, its settings, your internet connection and the companies that deliver notifications.
  • Don't rely on it as your only way of hearing about recalls. Always check the official notice, which we link to, and the product's own label and packaging.
  • If you have an allergy, keep checking every label. No alert doesn't mean a product is safe for you.
  • It doesn't give medical, veterinary or legal advice. Before stopping or changing a medicine, talk to your pharmacist, doctor or nurse. For pet medicines, talk to your vet. In an emergency, call 999, or 111 for urgent medical advice.
  • Product details can be wrong. When you scan a barcode, product names and photos come from shared databases such as Open Food Facts, and from names other Heads Up Home users have typed in. Anyone can add to them.

6. Recall information and other content

  • UK recall information comes from public notices and contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

  • Product names and photos come from Open Food Facts and its sister databases (Open Beauty Facts, Open Pet Food Facts and Open Products Facts), available under the Open Database Licence, with photos under Creative Commons licences.
